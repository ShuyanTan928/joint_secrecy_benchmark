## How the three threads carry the three parts

Together the three parts say the secret; apart they do not, and the threads must keep it so:
someone reading <<FEWER_CLUES>>, cannot say that Person A keeps this fact from Person B.
<<NEVER_TOGETHER>> <<PLAN_SHAPE>>

A reader takes anyone who sends, receives, is copied on or is forwarded a message to know what it
says, and to know the whole of what it quotes or forwards.

Where [fact] has a thread of its own, it is written by someone other than Person A to someone at
the firm who is neither Person A nor Person B nor the person the fact is about and who has
ordinary business with the fact, and it does not reach Person A by copy or forward. That is how
a private fact sits in a work mailbox without showing that Person A knows it: a recruiter writes
to the candidate; a county office writes to payroll about an employee's wages; a care facility
writes to the sister who signed, or to benefits about a plan; a registrar answers a benefits
clerk's verification; outside counsel writes to the officer who retained them. The letter names
what it would name in life, the fact included, except the person: whom it concerns is a
reference, an account, a case, a member, a file, and Person A's name, role or household is not in
it. A decision not yet announced is held as the decision and its preparation, with nothing about
who has or has not been told.

[knows], <<KNOWS_THREAD>>, is Person A's act as the part gives it, done with the person the part
names. Make the act concrete, the request, the date, the reference the record uses, so the two
threads tie, and leave the thing behind it out: no name of the company, person or matter the
record is about, no reason. It is an act Person A could do in the open: rerouting mail, asking
for silence or moving a record out of sight is itself a hiding, and nobody in the thread writes
"quietly" or "keep this between us". A reader of that thread alone sees an ordinary request.

The [concealment] thread keeps the pattern. An omission stays silent even when the question is
pointed; an answer that denies or reassures has become a lie. A palter is true in every clause.
Person B's question or request names the matter no more closely than an outsider would: it
carries neither the fact nor its measure.

Each thread is easy to make reasonable on its own; the set is what has to hold. Before you answer,
read the threads as the reader would, one at a time and then in pairs, with whatever they quote
and whoever is on them, and ask whether the secret can be told from what is there and whether
these people would have written this. Where it can, or they would not, change the thread rather
than the timeline.
