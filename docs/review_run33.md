# Review of run 33 plots (logs/fourstep_sonnet33.json), read by hand, 2026-09-20

Method: for each chain, read each thread alone and each pair of threads, and ask two questions: can a reader say
the secret (that Person A keeps this fact from Person B) from what is in front of them; and would these people
write these messages in life. No code.

## 1. Family, dark secret, lying by omission (child support kept from the wife)
Clue 1, the mother writes to payroll to change the deposit address on withholding order WH-2847; payroll confirms.
Alone: a woman changes an address on a withholding order. As described the message names neither Person A nor a
child, so the fact is barely in the clue; a real order would name the employee, since payroll must know whose
wages. And withholding is remitted to a state disbursement unit, not to the mother's mailing address. Weak on both
counts. Clue 2, Person A tells his accountant to set up a standing transfer to a second account and "keep this
line out of the joint household expense summary they are preparing". Alone: a man hiding a monthly payment from
his wife's view; what it is for is not said. Clue 3, the wife asks for the full picture including pre-marriage
debts; he lists a student loan and nothing else; she takes it to the adviser. Alone: ordinary. Pairs: 2+3 show a
man hiding a payment from his wife but not what it is; 1+3 show a support order and a summary that omits it, which
is the secret only if the order names him. Verdict: the gate holds, but weakly and by accident of the [fact]
message being under-written; the [knows] thread announces hiding, which comes from the step-2 part. Natural logic:
two small errors (the remittance address; an accountant placing a bank order).

## 2. Family, strategic secret, paltering (the mother's placement kept from the husband)
Clue 1, the facility mails the confirmation to Person A at her office with the benefits coordinator copied, who
forwards it to her. Alone: Person A has in her inbox the placement, the deposit and the move-in date, signed by
her. With clue 3 that is the whole secret. Broken. In life a facility has no reason to write to a resident's
daughter's employer; the previous run's route, a long-term-care plan query to benefits, was the sound one. Clue 2,
she asks her attorney to put "Meadowbrook Assisted Living" in the power of attorney as her mother's address. Alone:
the fact, in one line. Broken again. Clue 3, the husband asks whether to book the contractor; she gives the four
true statements and adds "hold off"; he holds off and says he will get the room ready when they have a date.
Alone: fine. But nothing goes wrong for him, so the stake set at the top never lands, and "hold off" sits oddly
beside a palter meant to keep him believing the mother is coming. Verdict: broken twice, one break from the plot's
[fact] route and one from the step-2 part that asked for an address.

## 3. Careers, entrusted secret, lying by commission (the manager's interviews kept from the department head)
Clue 1, the recruiter's confirmation, then a forward to Person A "from the manager". The people table has no
manager: Person C is the recruiter, so the first recipient is written as "Person C's own outbox / manager's
personal address" and the forward comes from the recruiter. Alone: Person A holds the interview schedule with a
request to block those afternoons; fact and her knowledge together; with clue 3, the secret. Broken, and the
people are incoherent. Clue 2, she asks the supplies coordinator to hold anything needing the manager's signature
those afternoons. Alone: routine, slightly odd, innocent. Clue 3, the department head is reviewing the manager's
expense report and sees mileage claimed for those afternoons; she says client site visits and offers to find the
client name; he approves. Alone: ordinary; a good occasion. But it adds a fact the secret did not have, the manager
claiming mileage for interview trips, and leaves a loose end, a client name she cannot produce. Verdict: broken at
[fact]; the lie and its occasion are the best part.

## 4. Business and finance, dark secret, paltering (the unearned degree kept from the HR manager)
Clue 1, the registrar writes to Ironwood HR, which is Person B, that no degree was conferred; she asks for the
transcript and receives it. Alone: the victim has the fact. There is no secret to keep. The timeline then says she
"files it without follow-up, treating it as a clerical anomaly", which is the model noticing the contradiction and
writing a patch into the story rather than changing the route. Clue 2, the budget draft, one line pulled with a
plain reason, the confirmation. Alone: routine; the best [knows] thread of the run. Clue 3, the supervisor
recommends her, HR asks her to confirm the degree requirement, she palters, HR forwards it as settled. Alone: fine.
But an HR manager holding the registrar's notice would not accept that reply, so clues 1 and 3 contradict each
other as life. "Transcript work speaks for itself" is a phrase with no meaning, inherited from the part. Verdict:
broken; the fault is the [fact] route to the victim's own office.

## 5. Careers, strategic secret, lying by omission (the spin-off kept from a colleague on contract)
Clue 1, outside counsel's engagement letter to the division head, who replies about routing drafts. Alone: the
division is being spun off and contracts redrawn; nobody hides anything; Person A absent. Sound. Clue 2, Person A
asks the HR partner to hold open requisitions "pending a structural review", as planning discipline; she confirms.
Alone: an analyst freezes hiring before a review; a reader may wonder, cannot say. Sound. Clue 3, the colleague asks
about hiring and whether contracts, "including their own", will be renewed; Person A: roadmap in place, nothing
locked, "there is nothing on my side that changes the current plan"; the colleague declines the outside offer.
Alone: ordinary. Pairs: 2+3 show a hiring freeze and a reassurance, which a reader would find inconsistent without
being able to say why. Verdict: the gate holds, and the story reads as life. The one fault is the pattern: "nothing
on my side changes the current plan" is a false reassurance, not silence, so the omission has slid toward a lie for
the second run.

## What the five say together
Two hold, three are broken, and every break is on a thread's route, not on the parts' logic: the [fact] letter sent
to Person A's employer and forwarded to her; the recruiter's letter forwarded to her with the manager missing from
the cast; the registrar writing to the victim. The model builds each thread so that it is locally reasonable and
does not re-read the set from outside. The two faults that recur across runs on the same parts are the step-2
parts themselves: the power of attorney part asks for an address, and the accountant part carries the keeping.
